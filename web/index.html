KNOTSCAPE IN THE BROWSER

A familiar toolbox, a new home.

Browse the original prime-knot tables through 16 crossings, enter a signed Dowker–Thistlethwaite code, or draw your own polygonal knot. All engines run locally in WebAssembly workers. Your drawings and codes are saved in this browser automatically; “Save workspace” downloads a portable backup.

Getting started

  1. Choose a crossing number and an alternating or nonalternating table. Names such as 8a11 use the Hoste–Thistlethwaite ordering, not Rolfsen ordering.
  2. Load a DT code such as 4 6 8 2. For saved KnotScape files, choose “KnotScape records”: 4 1 4 6 8 2.
  3. Braid letters a, b, c… are generators; capitals are inverses. Try aaa or aBaB.
  4. In “Draw”, click to add vertices and close the curve. Click a crossing to switch the overpass; drag a vertex to reshape the polygon. Use “New curve” (or right-click) to finish an open arc and begin another. Click an open endpoint to extend or join arcs. Select edges and choose “Cut edges” to split curves; Shift-click selects multiple edges. Double-click a vertex to remove it. Color, width and orientation controls apply to the selected component. Reflect switches every crossing; Alternate makes a closed projection alternating.

Calculations

Alexander, Jones, HOMFLY-PT, and Kauffman polynomials; determinant and signature; knot simplification, prime decomposition and table identification; representations into S₅ and branched-cover homology; hyperbolic volume, maximal cusps, symmetry groups, horoballs, canonical cusp sections, and Ford domains. Export diagrams as SVG or PostScript and complements in SnapPea format.

Polynomial variable conventions follow the original KnotScape documentation. Signed DT codes choose a handedness convention; a mirrored knot can change Jones, HOMFLY-PT, Kauffman, and signature results. Hyperbolic results are numerical, not interval-certified proofs. An unsuccessful hyperbolic solution is not a proof that a knot is nonhyperbolic.

Compatibility

Computational limits follow the original engines (49 crossings, and bridge number at most 5 for group representations); hard jobs can take longer, and can be cancelled. Some engines require a prime, reduced projection: use “Identify knot” to simplify first. The symmetry tab includes individual fixed sets computed from the canonical triangulation. LinkSmith (.lsd) drawings can be imported and exported, including open arcs. Close all curves before computing a knot or exporting a link complement. See the project README for the tested compatibility matrix.
